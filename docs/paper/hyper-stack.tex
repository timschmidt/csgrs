\begin{abstract}
A reliable geometric result must remain usable by the next supported operation.
Exact arithmetic alone does not ensure this: a result can lose its source
meaning, certainty, topology, or resource feasibility at a later boundary.
This manuscript describes a compositional acceptance contract for the Hyper
stack, a Rust system spanning exact-aware scalars, predicates, curves, meshes
and a modeling interface. We distinguish numerical evidence, topological
validity and fidelity to an intended source; formulate elementary set identities
and evidence-reuse obligations; and relate those obligations to inspected public
types. A retrospective case study recomputes 476 recorded process observations
for a proposed circle-support cache, including unfavorable measurements.
Warm-query improvements coexist with cold-query and representation-dependent
costs. Later library-suite successes do not establish downstream admission.
A rejected native integration and a failed external package resolution further
illustrate why acceptance must name the completed workload. The contribution is
a documented design and bounded empirical case study, not a proof of the entire
implementation, a universal performance claim, or a completed release.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Geometric software is used as a sequence of operations: construct a profile,
transform it, subtract another body, inspect the result, and exchange it with a
different tool. A successful intermediate answer has practical value only if
that sequence can continue with the promised meaning. An empty Boolean result
is useful geometry. A failed predicate, malformed operand or exhausted budget
is useful diagnostic information. Confusing these outcomes breaks composition
even if every arithmetic operation that actually ran was exact.

The Hyper stack separates scalar arithmetic, geometric predicates, curve
operations, mesh topology and modeling vocabulary. Its governing preference is
exactness, completeness, performance, memory, binary size, then source size,
within a declared domain and resource contract \cite{contract}. This ordering
rules out counting a faster error as a faster completed operation. It also
requires deliberate migrations when a change alters the represented geometry
or deterministic output contract.

This paper develops three connected points. First, semantic and evidentiary
closure are separate obligations: a downstream consumer needs both a usable
representation and enough information to interpret its claims. Second, cache
behavior and resource policies belong in that contract. A retained proof may
improve a later answer, whereas an exhausted attempt is not a permanent
mathematical verdict. Third, evidence for a local optimization must be kept
separate from evidence for a complete modeling workload or a shipped package.

The presentation combines a proposed acceptance framework, selected observations
of current source, elementary mathematical arguments and historical experiments.
These categories are identified explicitly. The manuscript is a working draft:
independent review, broader experiments and publication preparation remain open.
No new geometry benchmark was run while preparing it.

\section{Related work and positioning}
Adaptive exact predicates establish a classical route from numerical arithmetic
to reliable geometric branching. Shewchuk develops adaptive arithmetic and
robust orientation and incircle predicates, doing additional work when a
decision requires it \cite{shewchuk}. The relevant lesson here is that a
sufficiently justified sign can control a combinatorial decision; it does not
alone establish all invariants of the surrounding modeling pipeline.

Pion and Fabri describe generic lazy exact geometric computation, retaining
dependency structure and delaying exact evaluation until needed
\cite{pionfabri}. Hyper's use of retained structure and reusable evidence
belongs to this broad tradition. This paper does not claim that lazy exact
evaluation or expression sharing is new. Its focus is the obligation to carry
the meaning and scope of evidence through API and distribution boundaries.

Zhou and colleagues construct mesh arrangements and assign winding vectors to
cells, allowing different solid operations to select results from the
arrangement \cite{zhou}. This is an important precedent for separating exact
intersection work, arrangement topology and output selection. Their input
definitions and validation results should not be transferred automatically to
another implementation. The Hyper acceptance specification names its own closed
piecewise-constant-winding input restrictions and typed rejection cases.

The case study below evaluates one internal candidate against its recorded
baseline. It is not a cross-library comparison with these systems. A broader
comparison harness exists in the repository, but its complete claims and
measurements are outside the verification scope of this manuscript.

\section{What a geometric result promises}
\subsection{Representation and denotation}
Let $r$ be a representation and $\den{r}$ its denoted set under a documented
interpretation. Different triangulations can denote the same set. Conversely,
identical serialized floating-point coordinates do not establish fidelity to
an earlier symbolic construction whose coordinates were quantized.

For regularized solid operations, write
\[
  \reg(X)=\cl(\intr(X)).
\]
For regular closed sets $A$ and $B$, define regularized union, intersection and
difference by applying $\reg$ to the corresponding ordinary set operation.
The representation domain must also state dimensionality, admissible boundary
structure and orientation or winding conventions. This mathematical domain
is distinct from the subset currently supported by a particular kernel.

\begin{proposition}[Empty-result composition]
For regular closed $A$, regularized union with the empty set returns $A$;
intersection with the empty set returns the empty set; difference from the
empty set returns $A$; and both empty-minus-$A$ and $A$-minus-$A$ are empty.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the ordinary identities for union, intersection and difference. Regularizing
$A$ returns $A$ by the definition of regular closed, and regularizing the empty
set returns the empty set.
\end{proof}

This elementary proof gives an implementation oracle only after valid operands
and representation equality are specified. A shortcut must not return a
successful identity result before detecting an invalid index, unsupported
representation or retained construction failure in the other operand. Tests
must also consume the result: accumulation beginning at empty and subtraction
chains ending at empty exercise a different failure mode from one isolated
Boolean call.

\begin{proposition}[Affine compatibility]
Let $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ be an invertible affine transformation.
Then $T$ commutes with each of the regularized operations above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A bijection commutes with ordinary union, intersection and difference. An
invertible affine map is a homeomorphism, so it commutes with closure and
interior. Therefore $T(\reg(X))=\reg(T(X))$; combine these facts.
\end{proof}

The proposition does not prove that a software transform accepts every such
map. An executable test must name supported coordinate representations and
transform policies. Orientation-reversing maps also require the implementation
to maintain its winding conventions. Singular projections are excluded from
the proposition.

For a finite family of axis-aligned rational boxes, an independent equality
oracle can partition space by all exact coordinate planes, compare membership
of every open cell, and take the closure of the selected full-dimensional
cells. This exploits that representation's finite combinatorial structure.
Generic point sampling, triangle counts and checksums are weaker observations;
they cannot establish arbitrary set equality by themselves.

\subsection{Three independent contracts}
Every construction or conversion should answer three questions:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.69\linewidth}}
\toprule
Numerical evidence & Were the decisions justified under the selected policy?\\
Topology & Does the output satisfy the representation's required topology?\\
Source fidelity & What relation holds between the output and the intended source?\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A certified orientation does not prove manifoldness. A closed triangle mesh does
not prove that it equals an analytic sphere. Certification of values sampled
from a field does not prove a claim about the entire continuous field. Such
implications require additional hypotheses and evidence.

A simple exact arithmetic example shows the distinction at an input boundary.
The decimal rational $1/10$ differs from the exact rational represented by the
nearest binary64 value usually written \texttt{0.1}:
\[
 \frac{3602879701896397}{36028797018963968}-\frac{1}{10}
 =\frac{1}{180143985094819840}.
\]
Exact downstream predicates can establish facts about either rational.
They do not make the two source interpretations equal. Similarly, a valid
reimported quantized mesh supports claims about that mesh; it does not
retroactively certify preservation of the original exact geometry.

\subsection{Success and explicit non-success}
The acceptance contract distinguishes successful empty geometry, invalid input,
unsupported representation, unresolved predicates, exhausted resources and
unbounded output. These categories are a semantic vocabulary, not a claim that
one universal error enum already spans the stack. Kernel and public-modeling
adapters must preserve their distinctions.

In particular, an exterior-containing solid expression cannot be faithfully
returned as an ordinary finite boundary mesh merely because a finite subset
can be materialized. A partial diagnostic or preview may be useful, but it
requires a separate, explicitly provisional contract.

\section{Evidence and composition}
\subsection{Claims have subjects and scopes}
It is useful to model a claim as a tuple containing its subject, proposition,
input interpretation, dependencies and proof scope. A sign about an exact
rational coordinate and a statement about a designer's analytic surface are
different claims. Replacing the carrier or discarding an outcome wrapper does
not bridge that difference.

Current Hyperlimit source provides \texttt{PredicateOutcome<T>} with
\texttt{Decided} and \texttt{Unknown} variants. A decided result contains a
value, certainty and escalation stage; an unknown result contains the needed
capability and stopping stage \cite{implementation}.
The certainty vocabulary distinguishes exact information, conservative filtered
information and a policy-authorized approximate terminal. The strict policy
does not accept that terminal. Selecting the approximate policy alone does not
show that the terminal was consumed.

At the modeling layer, \texttt{GeometryOutcome<T>} pairs a value with aggregate
operation-local predicate certainty. Its \texttt{map} and \texttt{try\_map}
methods preserve that field, and \texttt{into\_value} discards the wrapper.
The accumulator starts certified and records an approximate terminal when
observed. These source observations establish the local API behavior; they
do not establish a persistent end-to-end provenance system for every model.

A required chain is therefore
\[
 \text{construction}\to\text{transform}\to\text{Boolean}\to
 \text{export}\to\text{import}\to\text{validation}.
\]
If approximate construction affected the original claim, a later strict call
must not be described as certifying that original intent solely because its
own consumed predicates were certified. An upgrade needs a new proof of the
relevant claim. Coverage of this entire chain remains an explicit open
obligation in the acceptance documents.

\subsection{Monotone reuse of available proofs}
For a fixed claim, let $E$ be available admissible evidence. A computation can
return a definite fact justified by $E$, or report that $E$ is insufficient.
If $E\subseteq E'$ and both are consistent and applicable to the same subject,
an earlier justified fact must not be contradicted by the additional evidence.
An unknown answer may become certified.

This is a semantic requirement, not a demand for identical cold and warm traces.
A sign established with more refinement may remain reusable in a later
zero-refinement request. Eviction can remove access to a proof; disagreement
between definite facts is still forbidden. Mutation changes the subject unless
identity and invalidation rules preserve the relevant proposition. Approximate
evidence must not become admissible to a strict request through cache reuse.

A cache contract consequently specifies key equivalence, object lifetime,
policy admissibility, precision, proof scope, retention units and reset rules.
Testing should cover cold, warm, evicted, disabled, mutated and concurrent states
where applicable, including strict--approximate--strict and low--high--low
precision sequences. A failed attempt that exhausted its budget is an account
of work performed, not proof that the mathematical question is undecidable.

\section{Architecture and resource boundaries}
Hyperreal exposes exact rationals, symbolic structure and lazy computable
refinement. Hyperlimit owns geometric predicate decisions and their evidence.
Hypercurve owns curve topology and retained curve carriers; Hypermesh owns mesh
topology; CSGRS supplies modeling vocabulary and bridges documented restrictions
\cite{contract,implementation}. These are layer responsibilities. Their presence
does not imply that every representation or operation is supported.

The inspected curve policy uses synchronous thread-local observation for
approximate terminals and saves and restores nested observations.
Its \texttt{Provisional<T>} exposes certainty and makes unverified value access
explicit. Transferring such a scope to a parallel or asynchronous implementation
would require an explicit transport of observation state; thread-local storage
alone cannot supply that behavior.

The modeling cache has caller-visible entry limits, FIFO eviction and
synchronous thread-local scopes. Its documentation explicitly states that an
entry limit is not a byte bound. Disabling the cache releases retained entries;
kernel and scalar caches are separate. These details matter because a single
entry can retain a large mesh or expression graph.

A resource contract should distinguish at least precision, total work, retained
memory and cancellation. A fixed 512-bit terminal says nothing by itself about
expression depth, integer coefficient growth, number of subdivisions or total
allocation. Similarly, a fixed cache capacity does not bound the size of its
keys or retained evidence. Where internal bounds do not exist, experiments
need external containment and must not report that containment as a proved
algorithmic bound.

The order of implementation follows ownership. Scalar identities belong in
scalar machinery; predicate certification belongs in the predicate layer;
topology belongs in the relevant kernel; modeling composition belongs in the
public modeling layer; quantization belongs at the format boundary. This
division avoids making each application reconstruct evidence discarded below it.

\section{Retrospective empirical case study}
\subsection{Question, candidate and method}
The evaluated candidate concerns operation-local reuse of circle-support
relations. The baseline uses ordered equality lookup. The candidate preserves
that lookup until a supported retained pair is reused with at least 32
relations, then activates an exact-rational index. It retains earlier unkeyed
witness priority and fallback behavior for symbolic or very large keys.
Distinct cold queries still scan linearly. The proposal is not in production.

The original experiment records a Ryzen 7 5800X3D machine with 8 cores and
16 hardware threads, rustc 1.98.1, an x86-64 Linux target and optimized direct
test-harness builds. The small-batch experiment uses no crate features.
Its schedule has seven repetitions, shuffled pair counts and alternating
variant order, with recorded seed 20261017 \cite{e46}. The candidate and baseline
retain the same focused tests, probe definitions and input construction.
Nine focused contracts per variant preceded the recorded cost runs.

Each small-batch phase executes at least 65,536 queries. Cold timing includes
creation and destruction of the batch cache; warm timing uses a prepared cache
and includes deferred activation on its first hit. Input/reference construction,
printing and process startup are outside phase timers. Cold does not mean every
process or scalar cache is reset. The measured calls produce complete ordered
rows and are checked against independent rational circle constructions in the
original harness. The small-batch timer includes those oracle checks,
coordinate-row materialization and row comparisons; it does not isolate lookup
latency. The shared-coefficient timer includes lookup, relation checks and
row-vector materialization, with its construction oracle outside the timer.

For this manuscript, the 140 small-batch process outputs and 336 clean grouped
shared-coefficient outputs were read from the retained archive. Each selected
member was verified against its recorded hash. The tables were recalculated
from raw phase durations and checked against the retained summaries. Group
totals were also compared with exact decimal addition at the recorded
nanosecond resolution. This is a reanalysis of historical observations, not
independent execution of the geometry implementation.

\subsection{Results and negative evidence}
Table~\ref{tab:small} shows the complete ten-size small-batch sweep.
Activation at 32 pairs is associated with reduced warm medians at larger
tested batch sizes. There is no universal benefit: at four pairs the warm
median is higher, and cold medians change in both directions.

Table~\ref{tab:shared} retains all three shared-coefficient controls. Each
group sums eight separate 20,000-query phases; it is not a single contiguous
timer. The original rejection rule required more than 10\% median overhead,
seven positive paired differences and a separately higher candidate range.
No grouped row meets all three conditions, but all three median changes are
adverse. A threshold not being crossed is not evidence of zero overhead.
The tables provide descriptive statistics, not confidence intervals or a
generalization to other machines.

The broader retained experiment also includes a fifteen-case support matrix,

In particular, its allocation reductions coexist with increased peak heap for

of favorable medians. The table uses the separate complete repeat recorded
after those local checks had ended. These controls reduce a known interference
source; they do not prove that the host was isolated from all external activity.

Two initial whole-library attempts stopped at the unchanged repository-space
guard and had no terminal success summary. They remain incomplete attempts.
Later executions verified the preserved binaries, dependencies and source
identity and ran both full library test executables in fresh output roots
\cite{e51}. Their terminal summaries appear in Table~\ref{tab:suites}.
The earlier failures were not overwritten, and overlapping feature-suite counts
are not an exhaustive-coverage metric.

The later library passes do not qualify the full Cargo target/feature matrix,
examples, integration and documentation tests, original native/report gates,
bindings or the external distribution. Accordingly the cache candidate remains
unadmitted. A successful local measurement is evidence for a specific decision
about a specific workload, not an automatic instruction to retain a change.

\section{Two wider acceptance boundaries}
\subsection{A native integration that was rejected}
An earlier 35-file native bundle improved several exactness and failure-handling
regressions, yet failed the original full-board and copper-neck resource gates.
The record rejects the bundle as a complete integration and preserves its source
and failures \cite{native}. Smaller improvements were subsequently considered
separately. Rejection does not prove the restored implementation's unfulfilled
mathematical claims.

The compared representations also denoted different geometry. Those observations
therefore cannot establish a performance-only speedup. This example explains why
both the output contract and the resource contract must be named before timing:
one cannot repair a cost comparison by silently changing which set is computed.

\subsection{A workspace is not a distribution}
A retained release audit created an external consumer with fresh Cargo state and
without workspace paths or patches. In that dated audit, resolving the documented
\texttt{csgrs 0.23.0} package failed; the registry response did not contain the
requested version \cite{release}. The quickstart was subsequently corrected
to identify the unreleased source-checkout path. This observation is about the
recorded registry state, not a live claim about future availability.

A coherent user path must install the documented distribution, construct,
transform and compose a model, inspect certainty, validate its required topology,
export, reimport and check the format-specific contract. Package membership,
license texts, minimum compiler, feature combinations and real binding consumers
are parts of that path. A local build or a package file list establishes only its
own narrower claim.

\section{Threats to validity and remaining work}
The mathematical propositions concern sets and homeomorphisms, not verified
connections to every executable entry point. The production descriptions were
checked against selected source declarations; they are not an exhaustive audit.
The scalar verification documentation retains open obligations for connecting
models, implementations, dependencies, cache behavior and termination
\cite{verification}. The whole stack is not claimed to be formally verified.

The measurements use one machine, historical schedules and focused workloads.
They lack multi-machine replication and a statistical model for environmental
variation. The shared-coefficient aggregate combines separate short phases, so
its interpretation is limited. Microbenchmark improvements do not predict a
complete native pipeline. The corpus must retain failures and all previously
certified behavior as capability expands.

Artifact hashes check byte identity, not truth. A retained archive can faithfully
preserve an erroneous program or an inadequate oracle. Independent mathematical
expectations, topology checks and representation-aware generators are needed
alongside deterministic replay. The broader historical audit also reports
27,093 missing physical artifact paths. This manuscript verifies its selected
inputs and does not claim complete historical preservation.

Open engineering obligations include ordinary strict-mode completion across
supported symbolic representations, end-to-end provenance, comprehensive typed
failure propagation, resource exhaustion and cancellation, actual C/C++, Python
and JavaScript consumers, measured coverage and implementation-connected formal
proofs. Motion-machine and continuous-field fidelity contracts remain separate
requirements. None is discharged by a manuscript, ledger, local test count or
successful PDF render.

For publication, the draft still needs assigned authorship, a venue-specific
scope and format, independent technical review, a distributable reproducibility
bundle and broader replication. This work provides a concrete manuscript and
traceable case study on which that preparation can proceed.

\section{Conclusion}
Exact geometric software needs a contract for the next operation as well as the
current one. Meaning, representation, evidence and resource feasibility are
distinct parts of that contract. The inspected APIs make several local
distinctions explicit, while the full end-to-end obligations remain open.

The retrospective cache case shows why retaining complete outcomes, unfavorable
measurements and interrupted runs matters. Warm-query gains can coexist with
other costs; library-suite success can coexist with missing downstream
qualification. Admission should therefore ask whether a change enables more
trustworthy completed work, or reduces the cost of the same completed contract.
