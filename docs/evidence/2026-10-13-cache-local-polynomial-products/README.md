The [source review](scope-v1.json) examines a narrower borrowing lifetime. A
private typed cache method accepts an owned left polynomial. On a cache hit,
it borrows the closed right coefficients under the existing read lock and
returns an owned product. On a miss, it returns the original left input
without cloning it. A cached-product decline is distinct from a miss and marks
certificate reuse, preserving the original outer retry.

The closed multiplication kernel follows the previously qualified operation
and insertion order. Its coefficient body uses rational arithmetic and
immutable structural comparisons; the reviewed helpers do not evaluate
expressions or call the expression cache. The closed right payload contains
no Computable owners. The typed entry point accepts no arbitrary callback.
The caller retains the right node, and the guard is released before recursive
normalization, cache publication or returning the product.

Holding a read lock across rational arithmetic can delay writers. Coefficient
bit sizes are not constant, and neither source inspection nor the planned
concurrency test proves a fixed lock-duration bound or wait-free behavior.
This is a scoped source review, not a whole-crate formal proof. Lock duration,
helper dispatch and code generation remain performance risks.

Left normalization still precedes the right lookup. A retained hit bypasses
the original depth/work counters, including zero budgets. A miss enters the
original recognizer body exactly once, without a second root cache probe.
Preparation recovers that body and all other baseline bytes after reversing
only the declared edits. Original memo insertion, opaque ownership, limits
and retry-after-memo-drop behavior remain. P4 permits stronger cached proofs,
never contradictions or weaker available facts.

The fixed scalar trial completed twelve preflights and sixty measurements.
All witnesses and representation inventories match. The [cost assessment](cost-assessment-v1.json)
verifies every command, raw log, binary and paired summary. The closed set has
68 timing and 68 allocation phases: two phases are slower in all five pairs
and ten in at least four; five are faster in all five and eighteen in at least
four. Directions and ratios are descriptive, not significance or application
speedup claims. Every sample remains, including large variation.

Conjugate-then-affine cloned queries are slower in every pair: median paired
ratio 1.054748, range 1.019898..1.420584. Warm queries are slower in four pairs:
median paired ratio 1.086002, range 0.996013..1.170242. Both save 570 requested
allocations and 22,800 allocated bytes per 1,000 inputs, with unchanged retained
and peak bytes. Those allocation savings do not resolve the timing costs.
Radical-Laurent warm is the other all-five-adverse row, with phase medians
16,119 and 16,359 ns across 4,096 inputs; this timer-scale result is preserved
without claiming a practical effect.

Requested calls and allocated bytes decrease in nine phases, by up to 2,000
calls and 96,000 bytes per 1,000 inputs. Retained bytes match everywhere; peak
bytes decrease in two phases by up to 96 bytes. No measured memory field rises,
and every paired delta repeats five times. These counters exclude native C
heaps, stack frames and allocator bookkeeping.

Multiradical-square cloned queries are faster in every pair, median paired
ratio 0.912608, with 2,000 fewer allocations and 96,000 fewer allocated bytes per
1,000 inputs. The other all-five-faster phases are rational multiradical-square,
radical-Laurent and conjugate-product construction, and quadratic mixed-field-
decline construction. All favorable ranges remain visible. Probe executables
grow by 2,848 to 2,880 bytes; production CLI size remains unmeasured.
