# Closed parallel certificate

This is an optional certificate for the complete unit-domain self-contact
query, not a substitute for normalization of arbitrary boundary loops.

Let a finite rational source have homogeneous tangent numerator H. Its affine
derivative is H/W². A nonvanishing K=cross(H,H') over the closed domain proves
that H never vanishes and that its angle is strictly monotone. Endpoint point
equality and positive collinearity of the endpoint tangents close the source
and its selected unit normal. Count roots of H_y with H_x>0 on [0,1), with the
upper endpoint excluded by exact parameter equality. One crossing then proves
exactly one turn. Strict K makes each counted crossing transverse in direction
space; counting distinct roots is sufficient. The test applies to either
curvature sign.

The existing singularity analysis separately certifies that W has no pole,
the source is regular and the selected parallel has no cusp, including either
endpoint. Thus its derivative is H times a continuous nonzero scalar. That
scalar has constant sign on the connected unit interval. Multiplication by a
negative scalar rotates all tangent directions by pi and does not change the
turn count. The parallel is closed with the same endpoint tangent direction.

A closed regular curve with strictly monotone tangent direction and one turn
has exactly one maximum and one minimum in each projection direction. Each
oriented tangent supports its entire image on one side. This certifies a simple
strictly convex image: an interior repeat would produce a second extremum at a
supporting line. Its only off-diagonal parameter contact is (0,1), whose tangent
cross sign is zero and dot sign is positive. These are the exact pair facts
published to the existing finite contact clipping and arrangement machinery.

No conclusion is drawn from curvature sign alone. A twice-traversed circle is
regular, closed and has strictly signed curvature, but crosses the tangent ray
twice and is rejected. A source with an inward parallel cusp is also rejected.
Unresolved predicates, flat curvature and mismatched endpoint directions all
decline this optional certificate. General bivariate discovery remains intact.

The direct seam, cusp, one/two traversal, reversal and policy regressions pass
in V7. The full quartic chamfer still exceeded its unchanged 75-second limit.
V7's bounded diagnostic reaches local chord-root discovery during candidate
regularization. Overall qualification and the implementation goal remain open.

The one-turn circle regression also exposed a separate exact singularity gap.
An identically squared cusp equation K²=S³ does not select the normal sheet.
Once S is proved nonzero throughout a connected range, K cannot change sign.
For K>0 the unsquared equation K+S^(3/2)=0 has no solutions. V7 certifies that
empty cusp set using the retained endpoint's exact sign; negative or undecided
signs and source singularities keep the existing incomplete boundary result.
This fixes outward radius-one circle offsets without certifying the opposite
inward sheet, which collapses to the center.

## Secants of a certified closed image

V8/V9 applies the same certificate to a finite chord whose two distinct
endpoints already retain parameters on the certified unit parallel. A line
has at most two geometric contacts with a strictly convex closed boundary;
these endpoints therefore exhaust its intersections. Neither point nor root
needs to be reconstructed in a joint coefficient field. Admission requires
the exact same parallel, zero additional displacement/translation, a canonical
normal frame, accepted retained policy, and both source parameters in [0,1].
The requested parallel range must also lie entirely within [0,1]. An exterior
range, a displaced point or any unresolved precondition leaves discovery to
the unchanged complete solver.

The exact original range clips both retained endpoint parameters. The seam
has two source names, 0 and 1; both are published whenever that range admits
them. Contacts are ordered by their exact parallel parameters, and finite
chord ownership retains the corresponding endpoint object. If the curve's
turn sign is s, an oriented secant from A to B has cross(chord,T(A))=-s and
cross(chord,T(B))=s. The dot signs are independently certified by the existing
tangent authority; uncertainty declines this optional path. Its new regression
checks these signs against exact scalar derivatives, the two seam names,
reversed source and chord, reversed ranges, empty/middle finite clipping and
rejection of exterior ranges. Runtime qualification of this secant change is
still pending.
