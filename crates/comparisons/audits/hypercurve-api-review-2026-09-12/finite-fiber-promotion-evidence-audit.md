# Finite selected-fiber projection and scalar promotion

Committed Hypercurve `ba836e13a98ec5e61087072d6eaf19432170d517` after `63dc97aba4fe578ceb11dee3bdc50ee2307f3884`. The original architecture goal remains active.

The nonlinear finite-circle audit exposed a shared scalar-domain dependency. Selected-fiber promotion previously searched a unit domain or an incident ray according to its isolator's position. Its complete continuation built an affine unit chart for exterior roots and mapped the result back. Finite projected-norm replay still used unit-only scalar and isolator constructors, and the general resultant fallback counted degenerate fibers over [0,1] even when given another range.

The common selected-parameter, resultant, reduced quotient and local-norm projection helpers now require an explicit `CurveParameterRange2`. They use the existing `CurveParameterDomain2` envelope authority. Local selected-fiber isolation accepts selected boundaries directly and retains those original boundaries for closed-range filtering. The duplicate Bezier-only envelope routine is removed. Candidate schedules retain their outward-envelope contract; geometric callers still own exact active-domain clipping.

Both scalar-promotion schedules now share one implementation over the root's retained finite isolator. The bounded schedule keeps its degree limits, and the complete continuation removes those scheduling limits without constructing or reversing a compact chart. Existing representation caches and selected-root comparison remain authoritative. An unavailable optional represented source point falls through to the selected algebraic projection. No new public representation, compatibility interface, or independently reconstructed Cartesian field is introduced. Production code is reduced by 145 lines.

The baseline at `63dc97a` records three failing regressions before production edits. Promotion missed exterior selected roots with isolators spanning a unit boundary; norm replay rejected a represented exterior root with `InvalidBezierParameter`; and a degenerate general resultant failed to certify a disjoint finite interval empty. The unit-domain root-count defect was also identified by source inspection. Final coverage forces both quotient and general-resultant schedules to preserve an exterior selected root and certify the disjoint case. Do not describe the original general-resultant baseline as a reproduced false-empty result: it remained degenerate there.

Four new regressions cover six finite-isolator promotions, sixteen exterior represented/algebraic norm replays, twelve degenerate projection/range combinations, and four selected-boundary root sets with exact endpoint ownership. Both policies and range orientations are covered where applicable. The first eighteen focused checks passed, including imported/refined scalar caches, conjugate rejection, shared root identity, high-degree fixed-distance projection, selected exterior intervals, affine/projective round trips and finite parallel component boundaries.

The baseline sources are retained as `finite-fiber-promotion-baseline.patch` with commit/source/binary metadata in its JSON. Attempt 1 failed compilation on two migration mistakes: a Bezier range consumer received a general range and a regression used a nonexistent reverse convenience method. Attempt 2 fixes those callers and passed the initial focused checks and all 21 public matrices. Its full regression run exposed two new failures involving independently encoded nonrepresented circle centers: a reversed analytic overlap and a correlated chamfer inversion. All attempt 2 artifacts, matching binaries and sources are preserved in `finite-fiber-promotion-attempt2-qualification/`. These results do not qualify the final implementation.

The consolidated finite-envelope helper had bypassed the unit interval's exact nonrational low-degree and Bernstein isolation authorities. Its general Sturm route could not establish the same coefficient identities. The corrected helper retains that existing path whenever the requested envelope is exactly [0,1], and otherwise isolates directly on the finite original-parameter interval. No unit restriction is reintroduced. Both regressions now pass as explicit focused checks. All twenty final focused checks, all 21 public probes/matrices, both all-target checks, both release all-feature builds and the three locked offline fuzz/UI checks pass on newly frozen sources. The corrected full regression run has **2,249 passes** (Hypercurve 2,017; HyperBREP 232) across 49 targets, five unchanged known failures, nine ignored tests, eight existing expensive exclusions, no new failures and no timeouts.
