# Constant-image trimming

Status: committed as Hypercurve `41c645b169dbcf420cd1800e436fbf5468b4ed81`. The original architecture goal remains active.

The positive-length trimming contract now treats a constant source image consistently: it contributes no curve piece inside, on the boundary, or outside the region. Constant spans never enter the positive-length intersection dispatcher, avoiding its shared-parameter-component blocker for a boundary point. Contact reports retain the original source-span indices even though active internal carriers are compacted.

Each trim operation prepares source spans and classifies their image dimension once. Path grouping reuses that evidence to bridge constant spans or authored curves at the same exact point. Constant prefixes and suffixes preserve a closed path's physical seam. An excluded positive-length excursion still separates retained paths, even when it returns to the same point. A constant spline span at a different point cannot bridge a knot jump. Grouping compares local selected parameters and exact endpoint evidence, without requiring global affine parameter reconstruction.

A shared Bernstein coefficient theorem proves constancy of polynomial and homogeneous rational images. It tests X-p.x*W and Y-p.y*W, including zero-weight homogeneous controls; it never requires rational reconstruction or an affine control net. One nonzero coefficient disproves constancy even if another coefficient is unresolved. Selected restrictions inherit the support theorem rather than projecting their selected parameter. Support dimension decisions use the strict policy counterpart.

Analytic supports reuse endpoint separation, circular-component and regular-domain rational-component certificates. A nonzero coefficient of the existing squared cusp equation certifies nonconstancy without sampling or root isolation. Squaring alone cannot select a normal sheet and therefore never certifies collapse. A closed quartic fixture with a midpoint cusp independently checks the derivative at the cusp and the coincident endpoint images, while the support certificate still proves nonconstancy.

The first test pass found two invalid regular-span fixtures: a fully collapsed circular parallel and an unsplit interior cusp. The tests now use the admitted exact homogeneous image for circle collapse and test cusp nonconstancy at support level while asserting that regular-span construction rejects the unsplit cusp. The zero-distance constant analytic source remains tested through actual retained-curve trimming. Constructor restrictions were not weakened to admit invalid regular spans.
