# Finite-domain chord discovery

Committed Hypercurve `047aa3540c27e0ba1ef8f020de8154a6f92ed5ac`. Qualification passes against the documented baseline. This is one part of the original closure goal, not a replacement objective.

The preserved public baseline (`finite-domain-baseline.rs`, executable/log/JSON) had 12 incomplete queries on finite exterior parabola fragments. It covers native lines, retained chords, and overlapping rational supports in both operand orders and both policies. The retained-chord cases are addressed here. Native-line/rational and general rational/rational discovery still use the rational pair dispatcher and retain its explicit domain limitation.

The recursive polynomial root authority now takes the original finite `CurveParameterRange2`. Direct projective roots are filtered against the retained endpoints; the shared Hypersolve local Bernstein isolator uses their outward finite envelope and then clips against the original authorities. Dense projection receives the same finite domain and still replays the original coefficient-field equation. Unit-only Bernstein hull and opposite-endpoint shortcuts run only on their certified unit domain. A unit discovery cover is reused only when it both contains the active range and has certified nonzero weight; poles elsewhere in that cover therefore cannot constrain a finite retained fragment. No curve is reparameterized or reconstructed on artificial unit controls.

Chord/rational discovery takes the active source range. Finite exterior requests certify denominator nonvanishing over that range and use the existing recursive chord incidence, point, and tangent replay. Collinear discovery isolates endpoint preimages and derivative roots in the same range, preserving all retracing branches, stationary singleton contacts, and positive-length overlap correspondences. The inverse correspondence also uses its retained branch domain rather than a unit preimage search. Constant maps retain complete source fibers through the existing common publication machinery.

Common chord/rational and chord/parallel dispatch no longer reject exterior spans before reaching these range-aware kernels. Region chord/rational callers now pass their actual retained limits. All renamed root-authority callers and dispatch-trace consumers were migrated; no compatibility function or alias was added.

Four new regressions cover finite rational isolated contacts (including a cubic with three exterior roots and a zero-speed cusp), retracing overlaps and inverse restriction, point-image fibers and poles, and a genuinely analytic finite offset. They exercise both policies, operand orders, traversal, selected endpoints, and exact point replay. The existing region exterior/selected fixture now replays its published owned carriers through common intersection dispatch.

Remaining scope includes other common pair domains, full operation compositions, normalized public-region construction, the documented five failing tests and eight expensive exclusions, broader algebraic authority consolidation, and computational measurements. This change makes no general timing, memory, or full-family closure claim.

The first full qualification (archived under `finite-domain-chord-attempt5-qualification/`) exposed three new incomplete compositions: retained circle vertex regularization, retained arc/chamfer Boolean replay, and a generated-chord overlap with an independently selected boundary. Exact finite isolation alone lost construction-owned unit-domain certificates when a smaller active interval was already contained in the unit domain. The revision reuses a certified covering discovery domain and its original component evidence; the existing consumers still clip contacts and correspondence against the active operand ranges. Structured point-incidence proofs are likewise reused before general finite-axis algebra. The original unit collinear pole guard is preserved. These are mathematical fast paths with explicit coverage, not compatibility interfaces. Final qualification must verify their repair against fresh frozen sources.
