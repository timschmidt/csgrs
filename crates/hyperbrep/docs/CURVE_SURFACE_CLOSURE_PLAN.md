# HyperBREP exact geometry closure and development plan

## Purpose and recommendation

HyperBREP can have a mathematically complete description of the curves and surfaces generated by a declared CAD operation grammar. For algebraic geometric data and algebraically specified operations, selected semialgebraic geometry provides a sufficient general model. A practical implementation should preserve compact analytic, rational, and procedural representations within that model.

The 3D problem requires coupled spatial curves, surface parameter curves, surface patches, and material topology. A solid Boolean retains portions of existing face supports but creates new edge supports where surfaces intersect. Even two circular cylinders can generate nonrational spatial curves. Adding a few more named Curve3 variants or forcing every result into NURBS cannot establish closure.

The recommended program completes a shared selected-relation representation, chart correspondence, complete intersections, compositional shell admission, and Boolean reuse before broad offset or fillet implementation. It preserves the current exact fast paths, native replay, and selected geometry. It extends the existing work packages rather than replacing the repository's development program.

This is a source and checked-in-test audit at commit `3cca6a894858c8b98497a3cfe81cff5e17e1a7f2`, dated October 24, 2026. No fresh Rust regression or benchmark suite was executed. References to existing capability are bounded by that revision. Proposed milestones and fixtures are recommendations, not completed implementation or measured performance.

The companion `AGENTS.md` is intended for `crates/hyperbrep/AGENTS.md`. This plan is intended as `crates/hyperbrep/docs/CURVE_SURFACE_CLOSURE_PLAN.md`, supplementing `docs/DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md`, its existing work-package status, and the stack admission protocol. [R13, R14, R17]

## 1 Scope and distinctions

### Spatial curves and boundary curves

A free curve in three dimensions has a normal plane at a regular point, rather than a unique left normal. A request to offset that curve must specify a plane, normal field, supporting surface, frame law, or a distance-neighborhood interpretation. The constant-distance neighborhood of a regular curve has a tube-like surface boundary; it is not generally another curve. End caps and global closest-feature selection matter for finite curves. [M3]

Choosing the Frenet normal does not recover the planar repeated-offset rule. With arc length s, tangent T, normal N, binormal B, curvature k and torsion tau, differentiation gives:

```text
d(C + d N)/ds = (1 - d k) T + d tau B
```

The torsion term changes the tangent of the displaced curve. Frenet normals are also undefined where curvature vanishes. Prescribed algebraic frame laws can remain in the algebraic domain, while general rotation-minimizing or arc-length laws need a separate representation and decision contract. Rational rotation-minimizing frames exist for special curve classes; they are not a universal property of rational space curves. [M8]

In a BRep, an edge is coupled to its incident surfaces. Its world curve and every face-local pcurve must describe the same selected image over complete domains. This is a stronger requirement than exact evaluation of an isolated Curve3.

### Algebraic completeness and analytic extensions

The general algebraic target covers finite descriptions of bounded regular-closed solids and separately typed sheets, wires, and contact sets, with algebraic geometric coefficients and semialgebraic selections. Arbitrary finite degree, algebraic endpoints, and coefficients that cannot be expressed by radicals are included. Every finite operation sequence uses explicitly specified algebraic or semialgebraic construction constraints.

Native angular parameters need special care. An algebraic number of radians need not select an algebraic world point. A circle support is algebraic, but an endpoint at angle 1 generally has transcendental coordinates. State the guarantee in terms of geometric data and selected relations, using algebraic charts where required. Existing exact analytic inputs remain valid under their own declared predicate contracts.

The core grammar includes regularized Booleans, section/split/imprint, constant Euclidean surface and solid offsets, shelling/thickening, constant-radius rolling-ball fillets, and explicitly defined algebraic chamfer laws. Transformations, exact trimming, reversal, reconstruction and native persistence are included wherever they occur between operations. Guided sweeps, arbitrary frame transport, geodesic offsets, true arc-length setbacks, variable-radius and G2 blends require separately declared laws and closure arguments.

Exact representation, an implemented decision procedure, and completion within a resource budget are separate claims. Effective real-algebraic problems are decidable in principle, often at high cost. Arbitrary computable real expressions do not admit a universal terminating exact-zero procedure. [M5, M9]

### Manifold bodies and all regularized results

Regularization takes the closure of the interior. It removes isolated lower-dimensional debris from volume results, but does not guarantee a manifold boundary. Two volume components may touch along an edge or vertex, and a regularized result can have a pinched boundary. A manifold-only Solid contract therefore cannot represent every regularized set result as one manifold shell. [M6]

Preserve the existing manifold-body API as an explicitly qualified domain while completing its general geometry. For the stronger all-result target, prove a faithful contact-aware BodySet decomposition or introduce a stratified extension within the same authoritative Model. The representation must preserve material membership and subsequent operations, not merely draw the same boundary. A new topology contract requires an explicit API/schema decision; do not silently reinterpret existing Solid invariants. Typed nonmanifold rejection remains honest, but does not close the broader target.

## 2 Exact result families and closure rules

### Generation rules

| Operation | Surface supports | Spatial curve and event supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regularized solid Boolean | Retained portions of operand surfaces | Original edges plus surface-surface intersection branches, isolated contacts and overlap boundaries |
| Section split and imprint | Retained supports, with cut faces where the operation specifies them | Curve-surface and surface-surface intersections with exact trim selections |
| Surface parallel | Selected normal-displacement image | Boundary images, focal/singular loci, intersections and self-contact branches |
| Euclidean solid offset | Face parallels, edge transition envelopes and vertex sphere patches | Intersections of candidate supports and closest-feature transition loci |
| Rolling-ball fillet | Selected sphere-envelope or canal patches, with required corner patches | Center spines, contact rails, end trims and intersections with remaining faces |
| Chamfer | Planar strips for admitted planar cases; law-defined ruled or other algebraic patches for curved cases | Setback rails, rail correspondences, end trims and intersection curves |
| Finite composition | Reapplication of the declared construction rules | Selected algebraic branches and events of potentially increasing complexity |

Surface intersections can be empty, isolated points, one-dimensional branches, coincident surface patches, or combinations of these. An API that assumes every result is a finite list of isolated points or rational curves is incomplete. Common components and multiplicity require separate treatment. [M1]

These are exhaustive generating rules for the stated grammar, rather than a finite catalog of every named curve. Types alone cannot determine the result: positions, parameters, tangency, singularities and material selection decide which components survive. Surface degree, curve degree, coefficient-field degree, selected-point degree, and output cell count must be tracked separately.

### A decisive nonrational Boolean edge

Consider perpendicular cylindrical supports:

```text
Q1: x^2 + y^2 = 1
Q2: y^2 + z^2 = 2
```

Their real intersection has two closed nonplanar components, with `z = +sqrt(2-y^2)` and `z = -sqrt(2-y^2)` over the unit circle in x and y. Finite cylinders with the first axis range `-2 <= z <= 2` and the second axis range `-2 <= x <= 2` retain both complete intersection loops away from the end caps.

The projective quadrics have matrices `A = diag(1,1,0,-1)` and `B = diag(0,1,1,-2)`. Their pencil has distinct projective roots `0, 1/2, 1, infinity`. Thus their intersection is a smooth degree-four genus-one curve; `(0,1,1)` supplies a rational point. The smooth-quadric-intersection theorem makes it an elliptic curve, so no finite collection of nonconstant rational NURBS arcs can represent a nontrivial open arc exactly. The equations and pencil calculation here are an independent proposed fixture, applying the cited theorem and quadric classification. [M2, M7]

Use unequal radii for this test. Equal-radius perpendicular cylinders satisfy `z^2=x^2`, so their intersection lies in the two planes `z=+x` and `z=-x` and splits into planar conics. That easier case cannot establish support for nonrational spatial curves.

Current cylinder/cylinder dispatch calls the parallel-axis specialization, whose first check rejects nonparallel axes. This fixture directly exercises a missing domain. [R4]

### Surface offsets and fillet envelopes

For a regular rational surface S(u,v), let `n = S_u cross S_v`. A compact exact normal parallel is:

```text
w^2 = n dot n,  w > 0
X = S(u,v) + d n/w
```

Retain denominator guards, the original source domain, chosen side and rank conditions. Normalization introduces an algebraic square root. Offsets of general rational surfaces do not remain within the rational surface family, although special rational-offset classes exist. A selected algebraic system can preserve the construction without eager implicitization. [M4]

Successive surface parallels can combine locally when their normals and regularity are certified. Write the unit normal as `nu = n/w` and the shape operator as `A = -D nu`; the parallel derivative is `I-dA` on tangent directions. Focal events occur where its determinant vanishes. A chart-induced normal can reverse when the determinant changes sign; a transported orientation has its own explicit rule. None of these local identities prove that solid dilation and erosion undo each other or preserve vanished features. Global self-contact and closest-source selection remain necessary. [M10]

A constant-radius rolling-ball fillet obtains its center spine from selected offsets of the two incident surfaces. For a regular spine c(t), the sphere-envelope candidates satisfy:

```text
|X-c(t)|^2 = r^2
(X-c(t)) dot c'(t) = 0
```

The selected contact rails, sweep sector, end trims and corner patches are additional defining data. Cylinders, spheres and tori are useful specializations, not a universal fillet catalog. There is an important rational exception: a canal surface with rational spine and rational radius law admits rational parameterizations. General fillet spines arise from surface intersections and need not be rational, so that theorem does not make all fillets NURBS-representable. [M3]

For curved chamfers, specify rail construction, pairing, setback metric and end behavior. A straight ruling between corresponding rail points gives a ruled strip, but the rails and their correspondence may be algebraic and nonrational. Arbitrary curved-face chamfers are not necessarily planar.

## 3 Minimum exact and practical representations

### Sufficient exact model

For the algebraic domain, use selected semialgebraic pieces with explicit dimensions and incidences: selected points, spatial curve arcs, surface patches, and material cells. Algebraic defining systems plus inequalities and branch selectors can describe the necessary geometry. The supporting ideal of a spatial curve must not be assumed to be one global nonsingular pair of implicit equations; local charts and auxiliary variables may be necessary.

Booleans and algebraically specified incidence constructions preserve semialgebraicity. Euclidean dilation by a ball is `exists y in A: |x-y|^2 <= r^2`; erosion uses a universal ball-membership condition. Quantifier elimination gives representability and decision procedures over the effective algebraic domain. It does not justify running a full generic decomposition on every ordinary CAD pair. [M5]

There is no unique smallest enum and no mathematically fixed minimum performant set. The meaningful minimum consists of capabilities: exact evaluation, selected-point construction, complete incidence, branch ordering and adjacency, trimming, full image correspondence, material topology, reuse, and replay. A single generic carrier can subsume these concepts without making their computational obligations disappear.

### Practical working representations

| Functional role | Preserve for practical implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Native curves | Lines and conics, including circles and ellipses, with exact selected trims |
| Native surfaces | Planes and useful analytic quadrics/tori, with exact domains and charts |
| Rational geometry | Polynomial/rational curve spans and tensor surface patches; exact spline span decomposition and shared coefficient fields |
| Construction structure | Planar lifts, extrusions, revolutions, declared sweeps, affine images and shared restrictions |
| General spatial branches | Selected intersection systems or selected surface images, with world and chart maps from one authority |
| Derived surfaces | Retained normal parallels, canal/envelope relations, and declared chamfer/blend systems |
| Topology and evidence | Correlated points, roots/fibers, branch germs, chart atlases, trims, contacts, material and provenance |

Keep existing fast paths as sufficient proofs of common requirements. Do not make conversion to one giant implicit equation the default. Add or remove specialization only with evidence for the same completed workload and maintenance consequences. Generality must come from a complete fallback and compositional admission, rather than a growing whitelist of whole-solid families.

Positive-weight rational forms are valuable fast paths. The general rational target also needs finite pole-free charts that may originate in homogeneous data with zero or mixed-sign control weights. Admit those through explicit homogeneous representations or certified subdivision/conversion; do not assume that all algorithms must directly accept every weight sign. Cancellation must preserve original domain exclusions and denominator guards. [R1, R2]

### Coupled world curves and surface charts

For each edge use, the complete active image must satisfy:

```text
C(t) = S_f(p_f(phi_f(t)))
```

The spatial curve C, face pcurve p_f, and parameter correspondence phi_f need not share one native parameter or independently materialized coordinates. They must refer to one selected solution and preserve complete interval correspondence, orientation and seam identity. Endpoint matching and sampled agreement cannot establish this identity.

Spatial algebraicity does not imply algebraicity in every native chart. On `S(u,v)=(r cos u,r sin u,v)`, the plane `z=a x` produces pcurve `v=a r cos u`, although its world intersection is an algebraic ellipse. Use rational/algebraic chart atlases, such as rational circle charts, or retain exact analytic chart maps with their own decision domain. The current oblique cylinder/plane path returns an ellipse without the paired pcurve form used for simpler sections, making this a concrete integration issue. [R5]

The restriction is deeper than changing coordinates automatically: lifting the UV line `(u,v)=(t,t)` through a unit cylinder produces `(cos t,sin t,t)`, a helix outside the semialgebraic core. Arbitrary pcurve lifts, angular inputs and frame laws therefore require explicit analytic extensions. Do not promise an algebraic conversion for every such lift or require globally rational parameterizations of arbitrary algebraic surfaces.

## 4 Current implementation assessment

| Capability | Source-grounded assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Spatial native and retained curves | Nine Curve3 kinds, including affine images, selected translations/rotations and planar lifts; no general nonplanar algebraic intersection carrier |
| Surface vocabulary | Planes, circular cylinders, spheres, right circular cones, ring tori, extrusion/revolution, rational Bézier and NURBS tensors; no general retained parallel, implicit or envelope surface carrier |
| Selected evaluation and derivatives | Correlated curve/surface points, selected partials and exact unit normals already exist within admitted domains |
| Plane and tensor intersections | Complete finite-domain polynomial decomposition and paired pcurves already exist for an admitted rational Bézier tensor domain |
| General intersections | Nonparallel cylinders, general rational patch pairs, broader NURBS sections, and derived carriers remain incomplete |
| Curve and surface incidence | Selected native pairs and plane sections exist; line/torus, generic curve/tensor and broad generated-branch consumers remain incomplete |
| Generic stitching | Important paths still demand materialized points and Real-valued trim endpoints |
| Solid admission | Curved shells still require family/construction certificates; general compositional embedding remains open |
| Solid modeling edits | General offset, shelling/thickening, fillet and chamfer APIs are planned, rather than exported completed operations |
| Native persistence | Version 14 retains polynomial branches, planar lifts, selected points and parallel pcurves; general spatial relation replay remains a target |
| Resources | Shared refinement/promotion scopes and archive input limits exist; complete work, heap and cancellation coverage does not |

The carrier inventory comes from `Curve3Kind` and `SurfaceKind`, not an assumption that every listed family is accepted by every operation. The spatial curve list includes AffineImage, Rotation, Translation, PlanarImage, Line, RationalBezier, Nurbs, CircleArc and EllipseArc. A planar lift can already carry a nonrational Hypercurve polynomial branch or analytic parallel. [R1, R2]

The plane/tensor fallback is a substantial existing foundation. It pulls the plane equation into UV, decomposes the entire parameter rectangle, retains isolated points and all admitted branches, certifies the projected image, then returns a spatial planar lift with both pcurves. Tests include an interior closed loop, disconnected and singular sections, a folded-image refusal, and resource recovery. Its whole-rectangle injectivity prerequisite and finite-fiber solver restrictions remain real limits. General NURBS and patch-pair support cannot be inferred from this increment. [R3, R15, R16]

Do not plan to introduce exact normals, selected points, or native polynomial replay from scratch. These exist. The remaining work is their consumption across broader fields, angular and singular charts, nonplanar branches, stitching and subsequent operations. Some older support-matrix statements are superseded by current code and the version-14 archive contract. [R6, R10]

The curve/surface dispatcher is narrower than the carrier vocabulary: rational Bézier/NURBS and planar-image curves have plane-section paths, selected circle pairs have specializations, and lines intersect planes, spheres, cylinders and cones. Line/torus and general curve/tensor cases remain unsupported. A new surface-intersection carrier must also support incidence with further curves and surfaces before it can serve as a reusable edge. [R20]

The key stitcher still requests represented Point3 endpoints, converts trim parameters through `try_real()`, and uses a represented domain. This is a concrete mismatch between retained geometry and topology consumption. The solid builder separately tries sphere, revolution, cylinder, prism, sweep, loft, planar and axial certificates before returning `UnsupportedSolidShell`. [R8, R9]

BodySet already represents the regularized union of potentially overlapping selected bodies; its length is not a connected-component count, and individual volumes cannot simply be summed to measure the union. All four Boolean APIs exist, but XOR currently performs two differences and a union without shared intersections. The general operation plan should reuse arrangement evidence across selections. [R18]

No exported general solid offset, hollow/thicken, fillet or chamfer operation was found in the public modeling surface at this revision. A shell topology builder is not a shelling operation, and a planar offset profile extrusion is not a general 3D solid offset. The existing plan explicitly assigns that work to WP10 and WP11. [R12, R13]

## 5 Staged implementation program

### Phase A Establish the contract and baseline

Owners are HyperBREP API/documentation and the shared admission process. Map this work to WP00, WP01, WP21 and WP23.

Publish a capability matrix that separates represented, evaluated/differentiated, completely intersected, chart-mapped, arranged, stitched, admitted as a solid, consumed by later edits, and replayed. Include native and generated instances. Record algebraic versus analytic chart domains, pole guards, singular support, manifold output policy, and degree/resource restrictions separately.

Reconcile stale support documentation with current tests and code. Preserve all existing certified workflows and their original hard fixtures. Classify open rows as missing representation, missing decision algorithm, incomplete component discovery, lost proof, topology restriction, format limitation, or resource exhaustion.

Decide the public meaning of nonmanifold regularized results before claiming universal topology closure. The current manifold contract can be a useful first-release subset; the broader target needs a faithful contact-aware representation and explicit migration if public invariants change.

Acceptance: every advertised row names preconditions, an exact-success witness, a downstream-consumption witness, and its resource envelope. A typed blocker identifies unfinished coverage. Do not convert a missing algorithm into a supposed mathematical impossibility or relabel it as budget exhaustion.

### Phase B Complete a selected relation from intersection to reusable edge

Owners are HyperBREP retained geometry, Hypercurve chart geometry, and Hypersolve selected solutions. Map to WP02, WP03, WP13 and the selected-parameter portions of WP06/WP08.

Extend the existing retained source pattern to nonplanar spatial branches or surface images of selected algebraic parameter sets. Keep original equations, both surface domains, denominator/radical guards, component selectors, exact endpoints, branch-event incidence, orientation, and world/UV maps together. A jointly selected tuple must not be replaced by independent coordinate roots.

Remove materialized-point and scalar-domain requirements from AtomicPoint, endpoint collection, edge matching, restricted-domain bookkeeping and generic stitching. Reuse existing selected points, vectors and derivative/normal authority. Extend independent-field and singular-limit capabilities only where the admitted workflow requires them.

Bind proof records to immutable sources and the relations they establish. Every transferred certificate must identify its valid restriction, transformation or reconstruction rule. Independently revalidate published models in a checking mode to audit bare proof flags.

Acceptance: a new edge whose selected endpoints have no stored scalar view has one spatial identity and exact incident pcurves; it splits, reverses, transforms within the declared domain, replays cold, and participates in another query and an existing or narrowly admitted Boolean workflow. This first complete path must exercise the selected endpoints; general mixed-surface Boolean completion belongs to Phase E. Incorrect interior image agreement must fail even when endpoints agree.

### Phase C Complete chart aware analytic and plane spline incidence

Owners are HyperBREP geometry and chart maps, Hypercurve arrangements, and Hypersolve algebraic topology. Map to WP03, WP04 and WP05a/WP05b.

Introduce a coherent algebraic chart-atlas strategy alongside native angular charts. Preserve directed periods, seam lifts, rational transition maps where available, pole/apex strata, and chart-specific domains. Complete pcurve correspondence for oblique cylinder sections. A seam is a parameter identification; it is not automatically a physical edge.

Develop general quadric-pair incidence using an appropriate algebraic method with complete classification and selected branches. Use existing analytic paths as accelerators. Do not add another isolated pair case that bypasses the shared relation representation. Extend complete plane/rational-Bézier decomposition spanwise to the admitted NURBS domain, with exact knot-seam deduplication and retained original parameters.

Complete the corresponding point/curve, curve/curve and curve/surface incidence consumers within each admitted algebraic slice. Include selected spatial branches against another plane or quadric, rational curves against quadric surfaces, and line/torus coverage. Return exact contained/overlap domains as well as isolated contacts; preserve all participating parameter correspondences.

Extend the underlying polynomial topology domain for algebraic coefficients, selected bounds, vertical/nullified components, repeated factors, whole boundary components and singular events. Preserve multiplicity separately from reduced geometric support. Retain the current whole-rectangle image injectivity certificate as a sufficient fast proof. Admit additional cases through certified chart partitions or branch-specific image topology where necessary.

Acceptance: the unequal-radius perpendicular-cylinder fixture returns both full nonplanar loops with both pcurves. Cutting those loops with the plane x=0 must return exactly four transverse contacts at (0,+1,+1), (0,+1,-1), (0,-1,+1) and (0,-1,-1), with correlated positions on both loops. The plane z=1 gives two tangent contacts at (0,+1,1) and (0,-1,1). Split at those contacts, reverse, replay and intersect again. Include oblique cylinders, degenerations to conics/points/coincidence, alternate frames, seam relocation, general plane/NURBS interior loops, and folded chart images. Perform both operand orders and cold replay before a subsequent trim or solid operation.

### Phase D Complete general patch intersections and arrangements

Owners are Hypersolve complete algebraic decomposition, Hypercurve parameter arrangements, and HyperBREP lift/partition/stitch. Map to WP05b, WP05c and WP06.

Retain the original three equations `S1(u,v)=S2(s,t)` in four parameters and both trimmed domains. Compare pullback/implicit or matrix reductions with direct selected-system methods on decisive prototypes. Eager global elimination is optional, not an architectural requirement. Every reduction must retain guards and replay candidates against the original surfaces.

Extend the same selected-system contract to general curve/tensor incidence and to a newly generated nonplanar branch meeting a third surface. These consumers must construct exact vertex fibers and trim parameters without forcing a rational curve conversion or independent scalar coordinates.

A complete contact result needs a global census of components, including interior closed loops, isolated points, boundary contacts, tangencies, singular branch joins and common surface regions. Projection exceptions, base points and nullification must reach a valid alternative or precise blocker. Numerical seeds and certified local continuation can accelerate work; local uniqueness at one seed does not prove global completeness.

Self-intersection queries must exclude only the exact parameter diagonal and authorized atlas identifications. An epsilon neighborhood exclusion can erase genuine near self-contact. Keep candidate/partial solver state distinct from a complete intersection graph. A successful empty graph must certify absence over the entire requested domain.

Use the shared graph to partition both face domains, lift exact cells and stitch shared edges. A sample witness classifies a face cell only after its connectivity and absence of unresolved boundary crossings are established.

Acceptance: rational patch pairs with an entirely interior nonplanar loop, several disjoint components, singular/tangent contacts, shared components, nearly coincident distinct patches and noninjective parameter images produce independently checked complete outcomes in the admitted domain. Preserve source identity through subdivision and replay. Explicit remaining blockers stay open in WP05c; they are not successful completion of a broader advertised row.

### Phase E Admit mixed curved shells compositionally and complete Booleans

Owners are HyperBREP validation, arrangement and material classification. Map to WP07 and WP08.

Implement a common validation chain: ownership/local incidence; whole edge/pcurve/surface agreement; chart/face validity; edge-use and vertex-link manifoldness where required; global and within-face embedding; material orientation; outer-shell and cavity nesting. Adjacent faces must still be tested for intersections away from their authorized common boundary. Two opposite uses per edge do not exclude a pinched vertex.

Existing sphere, cylinder, prism, revolution, axial and other construction certificates remain sufficient fast proofs of these requirements. A valid mixed curved shell must not need to match a whole-solid recognizer. Exact topology must not require an elementary area or volume expression; optional or certified numerical measurements have separate contracts.

Complete common Boolean preparation, coincident material-side ownership, selected stitching and shell assembly. Select union, intersection, difference and XOR from shared contact/arrangement evidence where possible, and reuse it across expression roots. Preserve BodySet union semantics, empty recovery, atomic outcomes and provenance.

Acceptance: publish a mixed curved shell outside every whole-solid recognizer, then Boolean it again after cold replay. Complete perpendicular and oblique drilling through a cylinder through the public BodySet API. Check pinches, hidden adjacent-face intersections, cavity contact and same-set/empty identities independently. Universal regularized-set completion additionally requires the topology decision in Phase A; a manifold-only result domain remains qualified.

### Phase F Add retained surface parallels and complete solid offsets

Owners are HyperBREP derived surfaces and feature coordination, supported by Hypersolve and Hypercurve. Map to WP10 after the relevant WP05c/WP07/WP08 contracts.

Add a retained surface-parallel definition using existing selected partials and normals. Preserve the positive normal-length branch, selected source point, original denominator guards, material orientation and local regularity. Complete intersection, trimming, persistence and subsequent-operation support for this carrier as it is admitted.

Keep separate contracts for a local surface parallel, Euclidean solid dilation/erosion, extended-surface or miter joins, sheet thickening, and shelling with openings. For a complete solid offset, construct displaced faces, edge transition surfaces and vertex patches; intersect the whole candidate envelope; apply global distance/material selection; assemble and validate the result.

Handle focal events, wall collapse, disappearing islands, cavity changes, closest-feature transitions and new self-contacts. Shelling must close opening rims and account for multiple openings and thickness sides. Invalid signs or thicknesses must not be repaired by absolute values or silent clamping.

Acceptance: exact analytic specializations; a genuinely nonrational offset of a rational surface; topology-changing erosion; multiple opening rims; zero distance; collapse; native replay and another Boolean. Verify normal-distance relations over complete selected patches and global retained-material semantics. A displaced face collection or locally regular patch is not a completed solid offset.

### Phase G Add chamfers and complete rolling ball fillet networks

Owners are HyperBREP feature coordination, selected incidence and topology editing. Map to WP11 after general Boolean and relevant offset support.

Implement declared planar equal/unequal-distance and distance/angle chamfers first. Angle laws inside the algebraic guarantee must have algebraic directional constraints or a separately qualified analytic interpretation. Curved-face chamfers require explicit metric, rail construction and correspondence; a UV offset is not generally a world-distance setback.

For rolling-ball fillets, construct the center spine, contact rails and selected envelope patch from one exact relation. Prove radius, side, contact and G1 agreement over full rails. Coordinate end trimming, tangent edge chains, convex/concave corners, multi-edge corner patches and collisions with unrelated faces. Keep analytic specializations where proved.

Every requested edge or chain must be accounted for as completed, rejected or explicitly superseded. Ordinary feature operations are atomic. A partial preview must identify omitted selections and cannot be committed as an ordinary completed fillet. Radius collapse, multiple solutions and continuous solution families require explicit handling.

Acceptance: an enclosure with several selected edges, a corner network, openings and wall thickness passes complete validation, replay and another Boolean. A general retained nonrational spine/envelope fixture must pass the same path. An isolated successful strip with unclosed ends does not pass. Variable-radius and G2 extensions remain WP19 work with separate continuity and parameter-law contracts.

### Phase H Carry replay resources and consumers through every increment

Owners span HyperBREP, HyperBRep IO and affected downstream packages. Map to WP01, WP12–WP18 and continuous WP21–WP24 work.

Extend the existing versioned archive as each new selected relation or derived carrier is admitted. Serialize shared sources once, reconstruct branch identity and proof premises, and reject forged selectors, changed source relations, cycles and oversized inputs. A saved Model is distinct from a complete operation history or intersection-completeness certificate; retain each promised level explicitly.

Extend existing operation scopes beyond root refinements and promotions to geometry work, coefficient growth, expression size, aggregate allocation, retained memory and cancellation, with units and worker propagation. Archive token/degree limits alone do not bound scalar reconstruction, validation, heap, stack or elapsed work. Test native and WASM behavior and low-budget recovery.

Keep native exact replay independent of STEP representability. Extend STEP only through conforming supported entity, number, chart and topology mappings. If exact target-format encoding is unavailable, return a format-specific limitation or use an explicitly requested approximation path. Preserve the source and its fidelity qualification. [R19]

Provide real native/WASM and optional CSGRS consumers for the admitted closure slice. Validate shared-edge tessellation and its geometric/topological claims separately. A triangulation can be explicitly adopted as a new exact polyhedral model, but this does not certify preservation of the original analytic source.

Acceptance: construct, intersect, trim, Boolean, edit, save, reload in a fresh process, perform a further operation, classify material and produce a separately qualified derived artifact. Check certainty/provenance and error distinctions at each public boundary. Performance comparisons retain the same completed geometry, evidence, output materialization and declared deterministic artifacts.

Use independent defining equations, algebraic root counts, topology invariants, material identities and exact negative controls. Compare alternate parameterizations, operand order, traversal, source sharing and admissible transformations. A nonuniform affine map preserves algebraicity but does not preserve Euclidean offset distance; only test identities whose metric preconditions hold.

Sampling, area/volume equality, checksums, or agreement between two wrappers of the same kernel are not sufficient geometry or topology oracles. Semantic equality and deterministic serialization are separate obligations. Preserve full-scale difficult cases alongside minimized regressions. Finite testing supports an implementation claim; it does not replace the mathematical closure argument or completeness of the algorithms.

## 7 Dependencies and completion decisions

Begin Phase A and the resource/replay portions of H together. Establish B before expecting generic stitching to consume new intersection curves. Develop C and the independent solver portions of D in parallel; their common chart/contact contracts enable E. Broad solid offsets in F and fillet networks in G depend on complete intersection, arrangement and material admission, not merely on evaluating new formulas.

The first technical prototypes should be the nonmaterialized selected-edge continuation, complete unequal-cylinder branches with both pcurves, and a mixed curved shell admitted without a family recognizer. These expose the main representation, chart and topology risks. A rational patch-pair interior-loop prototype then measures the harder algorithmic frontier. Estimate calendar work only after these prototypes establish actual costs and dependencies.

The existing roadmap remains the execution authority. Map each accepted increment to its existing work package and evidence ledger. Its review explicitly warns against completing generality by adding more whole-solid recognizers or isolated surface-pair cases. Preserve the pure Rust native/WASM direction and current policy of explicit API/archive migrations; permitted clean breaks do not authorize loss of exact supported behavior. [R13, R14]

Completion requires separate evidence for semantic correctness, representational reuse, retained/replayable proofs, and controlled resources. Required conformance workflows must actually complete under their published budgets. A typed unsupported result marks an open capability; controlled exhaustion validates resource behavior rather than geometric completion. Unbounded, nonmanifold, invalid, underconstrained and proved-empty outcomes remain distinct.
